# Beast Box ocean life art pack v1 (Cosmo)

Eight sea creatures with cute-and-creepy faces, models, sprites, normal/detail/emissive art and surface FX.
It follows the livingworld conventions (landforms/ README + gen.py, universe/planets, aliveprops): **metres, Y up, forward = +Z, creature-left = +X,
PBR + emissive, one mesh per material, near and far LODs, glTF 2.0 GLB, everything deterministic**. Additive: no existing file was edited.

Everything is generated by `gen.py`. No `random`, `np.random` or `Math.random` anywhere: every number comes from sha256 counter streams
`sha256("beastbox-oceanlife-v1|<REC>|<tag>|<counter>")`, where `REC` = genesis seed + counts digest of the recorded **ibm_marrakesh** job
(`universe/genesis.json`, or `$GENESIS_JSON`). It is a *recorded* hardware result, not a live quantum run.
`python3 verify.py` regenerates into a temp dir and compares sha256 of every file (byte-identical).

Look: round eyes, soft smiles, big heads and then a faint wrong note (extra-wide grin, too many tiny teeth-dots, trailing ribbons).
The neon wireframe is a **light layer only** (`wire_teal` / `wire_violet`: regular bands, lattices and rings). No branching networks,
no nerve/vein/neural/circuit structure is drawn on any creature.

## Run

```
python3 gen.py [--out DIR] [--jobs N]     # ~35 s, 86 files, ~13 MB
python3 contact.py [OUT]                  # preview/oceanlife_contact.png
python3 verify.py [--quick] [--write-manifest]
node /tmp/gv/vfull.js models/*.glb        # glTF validator (needs the validator at /tmp/gv)
```

## Sprites (`sprites/`)

- `<name>_sheet_128.png`: swim-cycle sheet, **8 frames per row**, frame 0 top-left, 128 px per frame. Rows: side view (az 0, pitch 6, creature faces screen-right) and 3/4 view (az 42, pitch 20). Seabird has 4 rows (flap side, flap 3/4, float side, float 3/4).
- `<name>_glow_128.png`: the emissive-only layer, same layout. Draw it additive over the sheet, scaled by `aLife`.
- `<name>_gba_32.png`: 32 px GBA mini sheet, same layout, 16 colour palette per creature (palette in `sprites/sprites.json`), no semi-transparency.
- `billboard_atlas_64.png`: 64 px cells, 8 azimuths (0..315 in 45 steps) per row; rows = fish_a, fish_b, fish_c, whale, jelly, manta, turtle, seabird, then three `school_*` rows (18-fish clusters of fish_a/b/c, one cell stands for a whole far school). Pitch 10 degrees, except the manta row at 45 degrees (it is flat; at 10 degrees every view is edge-on). Pick the cell by `round(az/45)` from the camera-relative heading.
- `planet_points_16.png`: one 16x16 additive glow dot per creature (see "Planet scale").
- `sprites/sprites.json`: rows, views, fps (fish 14/16/12, whale 3, jelly 6, manta 3, turtle 5, bird 14), px per metre, palettes.

The sprites are rendered from the same posed meshes by a **software numpy rasteriser**, not by three.js. One deliberate cheat: the fish side rows use a sprite-only up/down stroke (`swim_side`, marked `sprite_only_axis_swap` in sprites.json), because the real fish bend is sideways and invisible edge-on.

## Placement notes

### Sea level and depth bands

Sea level y = 0 is the ocean shader water plane. Depth d = -y. The ocean depth shader uses t = 1 - exp(-d / 12) (colour fade), and caustics only exist where d < 8 m.

| depth band | d (m) | who | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| surface film | 0 to 0.5 | seabird floats at y = 0, turtle surfaces, whale blowhole and breach | splash, ripple, foam wake FX live here |
| sunlit | 0.5 to 8 | fish_a, fish_c, manta, turtle, jelly (shallow) | caustics on; glow dim by day |
| mid | 8 to 30 | fish_b, fish_c, jelly, manta, whale | glow readable, caustics off |
| deep | 30 to 120 | jelly, fish_b, whale (dive) | glow dominant; scale `aLife` up with depth |

Per-creature `depth_m` ranges are in `models/models.json`. Seabird `depth_m` is [-0.05, 0.05].

### Biome ties

The `biomes` list per creature in models.json gives the habitat tags, e.g. fish_a: shallow_ocean, rainforest, beach, grassland, savanna; fish_b: deep_ocean, frozen_sea, jelly_geyser, crystal_moss; fish_c: shallow_ocean, deep_ocean, paper_marsh, sunflower_ring; whale: deep_ocean, frozen_sea, volcano island whale slots; jelly: jelly_geyser, deep_ocean, crystal_moss, celestial_peaks; manta: shallow_ocean, deep_ocean, beach drop-off; turtle: shallow_ocean, beach, grassland, heartwood_forest; seabird: beach, glacier, frozen_sea, volcano ledges, shallow_ocean.
These are design tags, not checked against the engine's biome table (see Gaps).

### School-instancing recipe

Skinned meshes do not instance in three.js, so a school is built in two tiers:

1. **Far / most members**: one `InstancedMesh` per species of the static `*_far.glb` (35 tris). `instanceMatrix` carries the **school** centre and heading; per-member offset and swim bend are done in the vertex shader (`glsl/oceanlife_chunks.glsl`).
2. **Near**: up to about 24 skinned clones of `*_near.glb` for the individuals closest to the camera (one `AnimationMixer` per type, with a per-instance time offset = `aPhase * period`). Swap by the LOD table with a dither cross-fade.
3. Far-field schools: one billboard from the `school_*` atlas rows (one camera-facing quad per school), and at planet scale a point sprite.

Per-instance attributes follow aliveprops: **`aPhase = hash11(schoolId * 131.0 + memberId)`** (computed on the CPU, in [0,1)) and **`aLife`** in [0,1] (life volume), which scales emissive glow, swim amplitude and speed together. A member's offset is a soft ellipsoid: direction from three hashes, radius from `cbrt(hash)` (uniform in volume), scaled by `(R, R/2, 1.4R)` with R = `school.radius_m`. Add a slow wander `sin/cos(uTime*0.2 + aPhase*2pi) * 0.15 R` so a school is never rigid.
Member counts and radii: fish_a 12 to 40 at R 1.6 m, fish_b 8 to 24 at 2.2 m, fish_c 10 to 30 at 2.0 m, jelly 5 to 25 at 4 m, manta 1 to 5 at 12 m, whale 1 to 3 at 40 m, turtle 1 to 4, seabird 3 to 14.
`schools_sample.json` holds a deterministic sample for a 600 m patch (centres, depth, count, heading, speed, life, phase seed) and heading rule `heading = 2*pi*h01(school_id, "heading")`; school centres drift along heading at cruise speed and wrap in the patch.

### Planet scale versus near

- **Planet / orbit scale**: nothing is a model. Each creature is at most a point sprite from `planet_points_16.png` (additive glow dot, tinted per species). Whale herds are the only thing worth showing at a few kilometres (a slow blue 3 px dot); fish schools below 74 m / 60 m / 102 m merge into one dot per school; jelly fields show as a faint teal haze.
- **Mid distance**: billboard atlas cell (whale 693 m to 2.8 km, school clusters under about 100 m).
- **Near**: skinned GLB with the 256 normal and emissive map. Hero reads: whale breach (clip + `breach_sheet` + `splash`), jelly pulse with trailing tentacles, manta glide over the sunlit shallows, bird bobbing on the water with `ripple_rings`.

### Whale anchor on the volcano island

The landforms island anchors (`crater_center`, `lava_source`, `geyser_vent`, `village_0..3`, `boat_0..2`, `lava_channel_0..2_end`, `sea_level`) **do not contain a whale anchor**. I did not edit landforms. `models/models.json` carries proposed slots, seeded from the same recorded counts, in island-local metres at sea level (y = 0), as a patch of the 600 m sample:

| anchor | x | z | heading (rad) |
|---|---|---|---|
| whale_surface_0 | 108.85 | -103.21 | 0.812 |
| whale_surface_1 | 22.31 | 176.60 | 3.016 |
| whale_surface_2 | -202.48 | -55.71 | 4.981 |

Use: blowhole at y about 0, `breach_origin = pos`, dive target y = -30. They are suggestions until the island layout adds real ones; check they are in open water (not on a village or lava channel) before use.

### Water-level and shader notes

- Place surface FX and the bird at y = 0 exactly; ride the ocean shader's wave height offset if it displaces vertices.
- Glow ramps up with depth: `glow = aLife * mix(0.35, 1.0, 1 - exp(-d / 12))` is the suggested curve.
- GLSL chunks (`glsl/oceanlife_chunks.glsl`): school offset, far-fish vertex swim, jelly pulse, emissive ramp. **Not compiled or run**; treat as a recipe.

## Contact sheet and verification

`preview/oceanlife_contact.png` (1900x1710) shows every creature near and far, sprite rows, textures and FX.
`python3 verify.py` runs 184 checks (all pass): glTF validator with 0 errors and 0 warnings, triangle budgets, clip loop closure, anchors, bbox vs size, texture sizes, unit-length normals, tileability (seam difference vs the 99.9th percentile of ordinary column differences), emissive coverage, sprite and FX sheet sizes, non-empty frames (ripple tail frames fade out by design, so it needs 12 of 16), README keywords, no randomness in the sources, and determinism (full regenerate, byte-identical sha256 for all 86 files). The fish normal maps carry a small shingle tilt on purpose (mean x about 0.12).

## Gaps (read before shipping)

- **Nothing here has been loaded in three.js or on a phone.** GLBs pass the Khronos glTF validator only; the GLSL chunks are uncompiled; performance and fill rate are unmeasured.
- All previews and sprite sheets come from a **software numpy rasteriser**, so engine lighting, bloom and tone mapping will look different.
- Fish side sprites use the sprite-only stroke cheat (see Sprites).
- Biome tags, depth bands, speeds and LOD distances are design numbers, not read from the engine. Biome tag names were not cross-checked against the live biome table.
- No whale anchor exists on the island (proposals above).
- The 32 px GBA sheets are palette-reduced automatically; a hand tidy would likely help.
- Not integrated with Phera's seeded ocean life merge on the site: this pack provides assets only. Hook up the sheets and GLBs on that side.
